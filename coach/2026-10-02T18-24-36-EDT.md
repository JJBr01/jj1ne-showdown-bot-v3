# Coach Review

## 1. What Worked

**The opening was textbook Wolfe.** Whimsicott Tailwind + Mega Charizard-Y Drought on turn 1 gave us *both* speed and power simultaneously — the exact board state we are told never to concede to the opponent. We held it for the entire game. Tailwind turns: us 3, them 0.

**Trick Room discipline.** Aromatisse set Trick Room on turn 1. We did *not* re-click Tailwind into it (rule honoured), and instead burned the 5 turns with damage. Their Trick Room produced zero KOs.

**Encore on the Protect-locked Golurk (T2)** was an excellent disruption: Golurk had Protected on T1, got Encored, and its Protect then failed — a free full-damage Weather Ball into it. That is a two-action swing in our favour.

**Whimsicott carried the game**: 144% damage, 3 KOs (Hydrapple, Pangoro, Golurk). Under Tailwind, a Focus Sash Moonblast user that moves first is effectively a third attacker.

**Protect memory was respected**: after Golurk Protected on T1 and T3, Moonblast was aimed at the partner, not the Protect slot.

## 3. What Failed

**The single critical error: Turn 5, Drogon attacked while a Rock user was active.**

Golurk returned at **2% HP**. The fallback engine chose an attack for Drogon. Golurk moved first and Rock Slide (4x on Charizard-Y) **OHKO'd our Mega** — a 49% hit that was lethal purely from type effectiveness. We were ahead 4v3 at that moment and had zero reason to take any risk. A 2% HP Pokémon deals **100% of its damage**; "it's nearly dead" is not a defence.

The directive "Protect Drogon vs active Rock users — even over securing a KO" exists in our own rulebook and was violated.

**Secondary: 3 of 7 turns were engine fallbacks (HTTP 429).** The one turn that lost us our win-condition was a fallback turn. The hard guardrails must be enforced on the fallback path, not just in Claude's reasoning.
