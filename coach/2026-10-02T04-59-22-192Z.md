## Analysis

**Record 40-80 overall; last 10 = 4-6.** The losses cluster in three repeatable failures.

**1. Self-damage from Earthquake.** Against itzjackalope, Garchomp's T6 Earthquake outright KO'd our own 1% Whimsicott while chipping Archaludon for 8%. Against jintah, T3 EQ took Milotic from 202 → 85, which is exactly why Rock Slide double-KO'd Garchomp + Milotic two turns later. Our standing rule only forbids EQ beside Kingambit/Sneasler — it needs extending to any chipped partner. EQ's spread value is near-zero when the target is a +6 Def Archaludon behind Reflect.

**2. Archaludon: chipping it again.** We already have two coach notes saying "burst or disengage," yet we spent turns 3-9 feeding it Pokémon one by one while it climbed to +4 SpA / +6 Def behind Light Screen, Reflect and Misty Terrain. After Dragonite went to -4 SpA on turn 2 (two Draco Meteors into Maushold and Pelipper — the second was arguably overkill), we had no burst left and the game was already lost. Killing the rain setter was right; the mistake was not preserving Dragonite's SpA for the Archaludon burst.

**3. Psyterrain's second carry.** Atlantean (Meowstic-F-Mega) and david7778 (Gardevoir-Mega) both killed three of our Pokémon with Expanding Force after we correctly removed Indeedee. Drogon was in both games and contributed nothing after turn 2. Kingambit is the only Psychic-immune body we have; pairing it with Milotic (bulk) and Garchomp (speed) beats bringing a 4x-Rock, Psychic-weak Charizard into a board that has two Psychic carries.

**4. Mega selection.** The byPlan table is now clear: Charizard wins sun (8-1), standard (8-4) and psyterrain (5-4); Dragonite is 1-4 in psyterrain, 0-3 in sand, and only pays off in rain and trickroom. Stop reaching for Dragonite outside those two archetypes.
