## Analysis

**Record: 37-75 overall; last 10 = 4-6.** The losses cluster into two repeatable causes.

### 1. Sneasler is our single worst matchup and we keep feeding it
Three of the six recent losses (thatsyntaxerror, Atlantean, fizwater) featured Sneasler, and in each one it removed two of our Pokémon by turn 4. Cumulatively Sneasler has 15 KOs on us. Dire Claw does 85–99% to Drogon and Whimsicott; Close Combat 90–100% to Kingambit. The existing adjustment says "keep Drogon off the field turn 1" — that is too narrow. The rule needs to be at **team-preview level**: if Sneasler is on their six, Whimsicott and Drogon simply should not be in our four. Milotic/Dragonite/Garchomp/Kingambit all survive a hit and can trade.

### 2. Psyterrain: we kill Indeedee and then lose to the real carry
We are correctly leading Kingambit and removing Indeedee turn 1 (david7778 T1, Atlantean T2, fizwater T1). But in all three we then **spread damage** while the actual Expanding Force user (Gardevoir-Mega, Meowstic-F-Mega) Mega-evolved, boosted and swept. Gardevoir-Mega did 100/99/66/46% in four turns unanswered. The pilot must treat the second Psychic attacker as the win condition the instant the terrain setter dies, and focus-fire it — including with Kingambit's Iron Head (super-effective) rather than Drogon's spread Heat Wave.

### 3. Trading 1-for-1 is not good enough
fizwater is the clearest case: we got a double KO on turn 1 (Indeedee + Raichu with one Earthquake) and still lost 0-4. Every subsequent turn we chose "attack with the Pokémon that is about to die" instead of switching it out. Against Sneasler + Primarina, that is four free KOs for them.

### What's working
Mega Dragonite in "standard" is 5-0; sun with Mega Charizard is 8-1. Keep both plans. Draco Meteor turn 1 into their Mega (Bootsandskins, erickermr) is consistently the right opener.
