Distilled from all 34 WolfeyVGC Masterclass lectures, Patreon topical deep-dives, and competitive guides in `knowledge/VGC Lessons`.
Every principle below is a concrete, executable competitive directive designed for live battle execution, team preview planning, and post-game adaptation.

## I. Core Fundamentals & The Action Economy [Lectures 1 & 2]
1. **The 2-Action Economy**: Double battles revolve around having exactly two actions per turn against the opponent's two actions. A wasted turn (Protect into a status move, attacking into an immunity, whiffing Sucker Punch, or a disabled move) permanently surrenders half of your turn's action economy.
2. **Move Resolution Order**: Moves execute strictly in priority order (-7 to +5), then effective speed order, with random 50/50 speed ties only when identical effective speed occurs. 
3. **Variance Elimination**: Pokémon damage rolls have an 85% to 100% multiplier window. Moves under 100% accuracy introduce fatal negative variance. Winning consistently at the highest level requires minimizing dice rolls.

## II. Speed Control: The Supreme Pillar of Competitive Play [Wolfe's Guide to Speed Control; Lecture 2 & 4]
1. **Definition of Speed Control**: Any mechanism that guarantees your Pokémon moves and acts before the opponent's Pokémon. Along with damage output, speed control is the single most important factor determining competitive win rates.
2. **Why Moving First Wins**:
   - **Removes Negative Variance**: An attack executed before the opponent moves CANNOT be flinched (Rock Slide/Iron Head), frozen (Ice Beam), confused, or KO'd before firing. Secondary status effects and critical hits only disable you if the opponent moves first.
   - **Damaged Pokémon Do Not Deal Less Damage**: A Pokémon with 1% HP hits with 100% of its base power. Moving first ensures your damaged pieces cash in their damage before fainting, and guarantees you finish their low-HP threats before they attack.
3. **The Speed Control Hierarchy**:
   - **S-Tier (Field Distortion)**: *Tailwind* (doubles speed for active side for 4 turns, effectively 3.5 usable turns because setter acts on turn 1) and *Trick Room* (inverts speed order for 5 turns, effectively 4 turns since it acts next turn). Prankster Tailwind (Whimsicott) is the most reliable setter in the game.
   - **A-Tier (Priority Moves)**: *Sucker Punch* (+1), *Grassy Glide* (+1), *Aqua Jet* (+1), *Extreme Speed* (+2), *Fake Out* (+3). Fake Out acts as a half-turn speed control by completely denying one opponent's action while your partner moves freely.
   - **B-Tier (Speed Modifiers & Drops)**: *Icy Wind*, *Electroweb*, *Thunder Wave*. These only provide value if the partner immediately capitalizes before the opponent switches out (switches clear stat drops). Never rely on them as your sole speed control.
   - **C-Tier (Speed Setup Moves)**: *Dragon Dance*, *Quiver Dance*, *Scale Shot*. Powerful because they bundle offensive boosts with speed, but fragile: switching or fainting erases all invested momentum.
4. **Natural Speed Insurance**: Always maintain at least one naturally fast Pokémon (e.g., Scarf Garchomp, Whimsicott, Mega Dragonite/Charizard) in reserve to dominate the late game when Tailwind and Trick Room field timers have expired.
5. **Speed Denial & Advanced KO Timing**:
   - *Delaying a KO*: Never mindlessly KO a passive or non-threatening opponent if doing so grants the opponent a free switch-in to their primary speed control setter or lethal sweeper on a turn you cannot match.
   - *Denying Entry Turns*: Time your offensive knockouts so their speed control setter enters the field on the exact turn your own speed control is ready to refresh or when you can immediately double-target it.

## III. Team Preview Mastery: The 36-Cell Grid & Elimination Technique [Wolfes Guide to Team Preview; Lecture 8]
Never treat Team Preview as casual — a match is often won or lost before turn 1. Instead of being paralyzed by comparing 6 vs 6, use Wolfe's systematic elimination technique:

1. **Step 1: The "Never-Come" Rule**:
   - Identify the Pokémon on our team that is heavily discouraged by their team (e.g. Sneasler into Psychic Terrain / Expanding Force, or Charizard into Tyranitar Sandstorm without counter-support).
   - Cross it off immediately. You have now simplified a 6-choose-4 problem into a 5-choose-4 problem.
2. **Step 2: The "Must-Come" Rule**:
   - Identify which of our Pokémon is mandatory to shut down their primary strategy (e.g. Kingambit against Psychic Terrain/Indeedee, or Whimsicott for Prankster Tailwind against Hyper Offense, or Milotic into Incineroar Intimidate teams).
   - Lock this piece in. Now you only have to pick 2 Pokémon from the remaining 4.
3. **Step 3: Sheltering the Win Condition in the Back**:
   - Identify our ultimate late-game win-condition piece (usually our Mega: Charizard Y or Dragonite, or a late-game Kingambit sweeper).
   - **Place this piece in the BACK (positions 3 or 4)**. Never expose your primary win-con to turn-1 Fake Out, status, super-effective coverage, or focus fire until their specific checks are chipped or eliminated.
4. **Step 4: Analyze the Opponent's "Must-Come" and "Never-Come"**:
   - What MUST the opponent bring to avoid losing on the spot to our team? (e.g. If they don't bring their Trick Room counter or Steel-type, Charizard sweeps; therefore, they MUST bring it).
   - What are they terrified of bringing? (e.g. Their Grass-types into Mega Charizard Y in Sun).
   - Plan your lead and initial target specifically against their Must-Come piece.
5. **Step 5: The Lead Pairing**:
   - Pair a speed control/disruption lead (Whimsicott with Tailwind/Encore, or a Fake Out user) with an immediate offensive threat that forces defensive reactions or trades (e.g., Scarf Garchomp or Dragonite).

## IV. Pressure Dynamics & Choosing the Move [How to Spot the Optimal Move; Lecture 4 & 8]
On every single turn, evaluate the board in this strict 4-step sequence:

1. **Step 1: Speed Order & Priority Bracket**:
   - Who moves first this turn? Account for active Tailwind, Trick Room, Choice Scarf, stat stages, and priority brackets (+3 Fake Out, +2 Extreme Speed, +1 Sucker Punch, 0 standard attacks).
2. **Step 2: Immediate Damage & KO Threats**:
   - Calculate min-to-max damage rolls from the Smogon calculator. Which opposing Pokémon can our active slots KO immediately? Which of our active Pokémon are threatened with an immediate KO?
3. **Step 3: Opponent Reaction Mapping**:
   - A Pokémon under immediate threat of fainting MUST react: it will (a) **Protect/Detect**, (b) **Switch out**, (c) **Commit Terastallization defensively**, or (d) **Trade damage** if it is faster or has priority.
   - The more obvious their Protect is, the more likely they will Protect that slot while attacking or setting up with the partner.
4. **Step 4: The 5 Core Moves on Every Board**:
   - *Option 1: Attack Slot A + Protect Slot B* (Covers incoming focus fire into our threatened slot while punishing their partner).
   - *Option 2: Attack + Priority Hedge* (Guarantees damage or finishes a low-HP threat regardless of speed order).
   - *Option 3: Double-Target Calling Out the Non-Protect Slot* (Heavily punishes their predictable Protect by deleting their partner).
   - *Option 4: Double Protect Scouting* (Used when ahead or stalling out their field timer to scout their moves safely).
   - *Option 5: Defensive Positioning Switch* (Switching into a resisted hit or Intimidate pivot to reset board momentum).

## V. The Three Safe Play Archetypes [Finding Safe Plays]
A "Safe Play" is a choice that advances your game state toward victory with zero or minimal downside regardless of what the opponent chooses to do.

1. **Offensive Safe Plays**:
   - Occur when you have faster Pokémon or priority moves that can pick up a KO on the active target while also threatening fatal damage to any switch-in that attempts to take the hit.
   - Example: Attacking a slot where both staying in or hard-switching into their resist still leaves the opponent in KO or extreme chip range.
2. **Defensive Safe Plays (Repositioning)**:
   - Protecting the targeted/threatened slot while switching the partner Pokémon into an immune or 4x resistant teammate (e.g., switching Dragonite/Charizard in on Ground moves, or Kingambit in on Psychic/Poison moves).
   - Advanced repositioning: bringing an Intimidate pivot onto the field to neutralize physical sweepers while completely negating damage on the turn.
3. **Macro Safe Plays**:
   - An unintuitive play that sacrifices immediate turn flashiness to secure the long-term endgame.
   - Example: Chipping an Incineroar or bulky pivot with a neutral move rather than predicting a switch, ensuring it falls into 1-hit KO range for your late-game sweeper later on.

## VI. When to Predict vs. When to Play Safe [When Should You Predict?]
Wolfe's personal read rate is under 50% — top players do NOT rely on constant psychic reads; they construct positions where safe plays win automatically.

1. **When to NEVER Predict**:
   - **When a safe line to victory exists**: If you have a commanding lead (e.g., 4v2 or 3v1) or your guaranteed damage lines win the game, **NEVER make an aggressive prediction**. Hard reads introduce unforced errors and throw won games.
   - **When the downside is game-losing**: If guessing wrong results in losing a core piece and getting swept on turn 1 or 2, DO NOT make the read.
2. **When to ALWAYS Predict**:
   - **Hopelessly Behind**: When down 2 Pokémon to 4, or facing an active Trick Room with only 2 turns remaining, safe plays will slowly lose. Only aggressive double-targets or calling out Protects can flip the game.
   - **Must-Win 50/50 Game Endings**: When neither player has switches left, splitting attacks cannot KO either target, and only a direct double-target into the attacking slot wins the match.
3. **Risk vs. Confidence Equation**:
   - *Predict only if*: (Confidence is High) AND (Downside of being wrong is manageable and does not forfeit the match).
   - Good predictions are hedged: being wrong still burns their field timer or chips a target into priority KO range.
4. **Anti-Tilt Against Hard Readers**:
   - If an opponent makes hyper-aggressive predictions and gets them right early, **NEVER try to out-guess them or enter a prediction duel**. 
   - Shift strictly to **Macro Safe Plays**: wide option coverage, Protect + switch repositioning, and standard fundamental play. Aggressive guessers rely on opponents tilting into erratic plays; fundamental safe play will punish their high-variance style as their luck mean-reverts.

## VII. Board States: Present vs. Future & Win-Con Denial [Understanding Board States]
1. **Present Board State Clarity**:
   - Never commit a technical blunder on the present board. Always verify:
     - Is Tailwind or Trick Room currently active, and how many turns remain?
     - Are any Pokémon locked into a Choice item?
     - Is Psychic Terrain active (disabling priority on grounded targets)?
     - Are Armor Tail / Dazzling / Queenly Majesty active on the field?
2. **Future Board States: Deny Speed and Power**:
   - *The Golden Rule*: **Never allow the opponent to simultaneously have Speed and Power**.
   - If the opponent possesses both speed control and high offensive output, you are forced into purely reactive, defensive guessing.
   - Your primary objective on every board is to **deny their most dangerous future board state**:
     - Knock out their speed control setter before it sets Tailwind/Trick Room.
     - Eliminate their setup sweeper before it acquires boosts.
     - Stall out the final turn of their weather/terrain/speed control with Protect + defensive pivots.

## VIII. Matchup Evaluation & Archetype Game Plans [Archetypes, July 2026; How to Tell if Matchup is Good/Bad]
1. **Good Matchup Protocol**:
   - Focus on low-variance, safe plays. Take 0 unnecessary risks. Stick to fundamental damage trading and win-con sheltering.
2. **Bad Matchup Protocol**:
   - Do not play passively — playing safe in a bad matchup leads to a slow, guaranteed loss.
   - Identify the missing fundamental (e.g., lack of speed control, or inability to wall their primary carry).
   - Take a targeted, calculated risk early (e.g. double-targeting their key carry on turn 1) to dismantle their game plan.
3. **Archetype Execution**:
   - **Hyper Offense (Tailwind + Frail Hitters)**: Survive the initial burst. Use Protect, Fake Out, and defensive switches to burn their 4 Tailwind turns. Once Tailwind expires, our naturally bulkier pieces and priority sweep them.
   - **Hard Trick Room (Indeedee + Hatterene / Ursaluna / Torkoal)**:
     - Against Psychic Terrain: Lead Kingambit; use Kowtow Cleave (immune to Expanding Force, hits setters super-effectively). Whimsicott Prankster moves are blocked by Dark types and Psychic Terrain.
     - Outside Psychic Terrain: Prankster Encore onto the TR setter immediately after TR goes up locks them into TR and reverses it.
   - **Weather Teams (Rain / Sand / Sun)**:
     - Control the weather setter. In Sand: Protect Mega Charizard from Tyranitar Rock Slide; rely on Garchomp Earthquake and Milotic Scald.
     - Against Rain: Mega Charizard Y's Drought resets weather to Sun, cutting Water damage by 50% and activating Solar Beam instantly.
   - **Balance & Bulky Offense (Incineroar / Rillaboom / Urshifu)**:
     - Exploit Milotic's Competitive ability (+2 Sp. Atk on Intimidate entry).
     - Protect our Mega Dragonite's Multiscale at full HP from casual chip so it can absorb any hit and retaliate with devastating Dragon/Flying coverage.

## IX. Best-of-3 & Rematch Scouting Adjustments [How to Adjust During Bo3]
1. **Deductions From Turn 1**:
   - Confirm speed tiers: Did their Pokémon move before or after our standard speed tiers? Update their shadow speed tier (Scarf vs standard).
   - Identify items: Did they take Life Orb recoil? Did they survive a lethal roll with Focus Sash? Did they take reduced special damage with Assault Vest?
2. **Rematch Hedging**:
   - If an opponent suffered a disastrous turn-1 blowout in game 1, they will almost certainly change their lead in game 2.
   - Do not make a 100% all-in read on a single new lead. Choose a lead pairing that is **65/35 favored against their expected new adjustment while still remaining 50/50+ against their game 1 lead**.
